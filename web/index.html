Each seller is paid its marginal contribution to welfare. What no seller's contribution claims stays behind and goes to liquidity providers.

With a single seller on the dominant side there is nothing to redistribute — that seller's marginal contribution is the entire welfare and it is paid all of it. Surplus appears only when sellers compete.

On Otter the searcher's swap reverts at every capital level tested. Joining the batch does not help: its order clears at the price that existed before the batch, which its own front-run did not create. Both pools are zero-fee, which flatters the attacker — on a 0.30% pool it would pay fees on both legs and net less.

Cost is superlinear: memory expansion grows with the square of the words allocated, so a straight line fitted over small batches predicts 726 orders and overstates capacity by about 13%. Most of the per-order cost is two ERC-20 transfers, not the mechanism.

What this does not do

  • Every guarantee assumes the consensus layer resists censorship. A testnet does not provide that.
  • The contract checks that an outcome is feasible and that no seller is underpaid relative to its own bid. It does not check that the allocation maximises welfare — the solver asserts that.
  • The pool must hold one full-range position at zero fee, or the realised swap stops matching the curve the batch was solved against.
  • ERC-20 pairs only. No native ETH, no contract wallets.
  • Both headline numbers are upper bounds: the attack figure assumes zero fees, and the redistribution figure assumes sellers who accept any price.
