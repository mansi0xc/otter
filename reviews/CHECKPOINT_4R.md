## Outcome

[The new pure inspector](../web/src/protocol/minimumFeasibility.ts) distinguishes
two questions: whether any integer output payments can meet the original signed
per-record minima for a fill, and whether a particular pivot rule is funded.
It recomputes 4Q from bound original inputs, then checks the least sum of original
whole-unit minima at **every reachable retained input quantity** for the original
batch and every record/address removal. A quantity dynamic program and separate
Cartesian enumeration must agree. No allocation/payment policy is adopted.

The following actual stored-order/core cases expose different failures. Budgets,
fills and outputs are raw underlying asset units, not human-display tokens.
Asks are original WAD-valued reservation rates; exact welfare uses WAD-scaled raw
output numerators. All openings are the existing zero-fee full-range model.

| Tick -6000, budget-1 ask WAD/10 plus budget-2 ask 2WAD/5 | F(0..3)=0,0,1,1; unrestricted choice `[1,1]` at input 2 needs minimum sum 2, while `[0,2]` needs 1 | Same-input delivery can be feasible with different fills and higher exact report cost |

For the last row, unrestricted exact welfare is WAD/2 at `[1,1]`:
output WAD minus report cost WAD/2. `[0,2]` costs 4WAD/5 and has welfare WAD/5.
Its single filled record needs one whole output unit, so it is funded by the
same one-unit core output. The exact welfare reduction is **3WAD/10**. The
unrestricted three-unit fill costs 9WAD/10 with output one and welfare WAD/10;
it does not improve on `[1,1]`. The inspector reports the same-input feasibility
difference without switching the production allocation to `[0,2]`.

The original positive-ask split case also reproduces for actual native input.
The tick -6000 case uses an actual native/core pool initialized at that price;
the book snapshots and capital weights are generated by the existing contracts.
Node model tests use separately recomputed synthetic content/anchors for that
price, explicitly without claiming RPC/book authentication.

These results are scoped to the represented one-sided, single-swap, output-backed
integer model. No extra backing, opposing-side netting or changed delivery
representation is assumed. They do not prove universal Otter impossibility,
profitable production exploitation or that the paper's continuous result is
incorrect. They do show why payment rounding or address grouping alone cannot
make every original welfare fill deliverable under the existing signed minima.

## Complete minimum calculation

For a fixed fill vector x, let
`m_i(x_i)=ceil(ask_i*x_i/WAD)`. Integer nonnegative payments meeting every original
per-record minimum and backed solely by the represented output F(sum x) exist
**exactly when sum m_i(x_i) <= F(sum x)**. Necessity follows by summing the minimum
inequalities; sufficiency follows by assigning the minima and leaving any
remainder. This is arithmetic existence before extra fees/rewards or other
obligations, not an adopted payment policy, incentive proof or future token
delivery guarantee.

For each case, the dynamic program starts with M_0(0)=0 and uses:

```text
M_j(q) = min over 0 <= x <= original budget_j, x <= q:
         M_(j-1)(q-x) + ceil(original ask_j * x / WAD)
```

Every reachable raw input from zero through the sum of retained original budgets
has a row. Equal minima prefer lower exact unrounded report cost, then greater
fill in ascending ask/order-hash priority. Costs/minima are additive, so keeping
the best prefix at each quantity preserves that diagnostic objective. A separate
Cartesian enumerator visits all retained original budget vectors, without using
DP states or cheapest linear-cost prefixes, and must match every minimum, cost
and aligned fill. Solidity uses a third enumerator with its own comparisons.

The frontier is **not welfare maximization on a new feasible set**. Its primary
objective minimizes required whole-unit payout; reporting that fill's welfare
does not prove it maximizes welfare among all feasible vectors. Choosing such a
report-dependent allocation domain/objective would require a separate complete
mechanism and incentive analysis. No grouped minimum replaces per-record delivery.
Address omissions remain hypotheses, without beneficial-owner/alias/LP removal.

Each original/removal case preserves kind/address, omitted and retained original
indices and retained-order hash. Each quantity row retains actual opening output,
minimum-payout fill, sum of original minima, aggregate rounded exact cost, exact
unrounded cost, **signed** welfare, positive shortfall and numeric feasibility.
Impossible rows remain present rather than being clipped or dropped. The case
also records complete vector count, DP transition count, the 4Q research input/
fill's minimum and shortfall, the least minimum and feasibility at that same
input, and whether any positive-input/positive-output feasible allocation exists.
Zero-input and empty retained cases are explicit; a zero-output spend is not
promoted to the positive-output flag.

The inspector first reruns `researchBoundOneSidedBatch`. It accepts no saved
calculation/hash as proof. All 4P/4Q original-content, fully consumed prefix,
explicit zero-row, one-sided and work checks apply before the new algorithms.
The same full original output table and price limit are used for all cases.
Original signed quantities/asks remain intact. The limits stay **1–8 records**,
**0–64 original raw input units** and **100,000 original budget vectors**. At
most 17 cases return at most 1,105 quantity rows; each fill has at most eight
entries. These are laboratory bounds, not production admission or measured
verifier capacity. Large/partial/unsupported/mixed domains fail rather than be
sampled, narrowed, rescaled or replaced with a report restriction.

The nested result objects/arrays are detached and frozen. The versioned
`OtterSignedMinimumFrontier/v1` ABI hash binds normalized cases/rows to the
recomputed 4Q research hash, which already binds original context/calculation.
This is calculation identity, not an accepted on-chain outcome witness. Mutable
input frames require revalidation when reused. The pure step adds no RPC and
authenticates no new anchor, current custody/lifecycle, historical signature,
consensus/finality or future settlement. The bridge uses the existing 4O capture
first, with synthetic transport/block hashes.
